For people responsible for more than one palm

A manager should not have to reconstruct the property from old emails.

I work with property managers, HOA boards, apartment communities, commercial sites, schools, campuses, and public-property contacts.

Agree on the property

We identify which palms are included, how I can access them, and what the manager or board needs from the report.

Review what is already known

I ask for earlier photographs, invoices, service history, and current concerns when those records exist.

Give each palm an identity

A stable ID ties the location, photographs, notes, and future visits to the right palm.

What I can do

Start with an inventory or a return visit.

The job can be one-time or recurring. Treatment is not a requirement.

Initial palm inventory

Palm IDs, locations, dated photographs, field notes, and the property's current priorities.

Return visits

Comparable photographs and updated notes showing which palms have changed.

Contractor-work records

Before-and-after photographs plus the service records the contractor supplies.

Decline and loss history

Urgent observations, contractor handoffs, removals, confirmed losses, and replacements.

What the client receives

Palm-by-palm detail and a property summary.

The exact deliverables are agreed before work begins.

Palm register

IDs, locations, species where supportable, baseline photographs, field observations, and status.

Priority summary

A straightforward list of what needs attention now, what can wait, and who owns the next step.

Written findings

Reported history, what I observed, remaining questions, recommendations, and a suggested review interval.

Work history

Supplied treatment information, photographs of completed contractor work, removals, losses, and replacements.
