Annual Palm Stewardship Program

A practical path from walkthrough to annual planning.

The first conversation is usually 15–20 minutes. A complete inventory or report is paid work, scoped after I understand the property.

1. Property walkthrough

Confirm palm count, species, access, known concerns, landscape responsibilities, and management objectives.

2. Paid palm portfolio baseline

Assign IDs, establish locations, create repeatable photographs, document visible condition, and identify priorities.

3. Stewardship plan

Set palm nutrition and health priorities, identify watering or irrigation concerns, and plan preventive protection, treatment when appropriate, and escalation.

4. Recurring stewardship

Schedule hands-on visits, appropriate care, comparable photographs, treatment and work history, and management reporting.

5. Response coordination

Organize contractor questions, removal documentation, access, loss records, sourcing, and replacement planning when required.

6. Annual planning

Prepare a concise portfolio summary for preservation, removal, replacement, operational priorities, and budgeting.

Once appointment windows are configured, walkthrough and service times will be offered only within them; immediate or continuous owner availability is not promised.

Observation and response

Visible conditions guide the next step.

I separate what I observe from reported history and suspected causes, then explain the response that fits the palm and property.

Palm condition and pest activity can change between visits. Monitoring and treatment reduce risk but cannot guarantee survival or pest exclusion. Pruning, climbing, removal, structural-risk assessment, laboratory diagnosis, unrelated landscape work, and unscheduled visits are outside the annual scope unless separately authorized and legally provided.

Existing property teams

SDPP does not need to replace the landscape team.

The stewardship role creates continuity around the palms, maintains the record, performs agreed licensed work, and coordinates specialized action when needed. I can work alongside existing landscapers, arborists, tree companies, plant-health providers, and other qualified contractors.

Coordination does not imply supervision or contractual authority over another professional. I cannot verify work hidden from view or certify workmanship, structural safety, code compliance, pesticide efficacy, contractor licensing, or outcomes.
