One service model across the life of a palm

SDPP connects the starting record, recurring observation, protection questions, outside work, loss, and replacement history. The same practical system serves homeowners, HOAs and property managers, commercial and institutional properties, and urban-forestry or public-agency partners that need palm-specific evidence.

The scale can be one important palm, a managed property, or a multi-property portfolio. The structure stays simple: identify the palm, establish the baseline, record change, preserve decisions, and document the outcome.

1. Monitoring & Documentation

A dated record that makes change visible and keeps property history usable.

Baseline

  • Numbered palm inventory
  • Species and location notes where reasonably supportable
  • Standardized full-palm and crown photographs
  • Visible-condition observations and priority categories

Monitoring

  • Quarterly, semiannual, or annual repeat visits
  • Consistent palm identifiers and photographic views
  • Side-by-side comparisons and notable-change summaries
  • Updated priorities and follow-up dates

Records

  • Palm-by-palm or portfolio reports
  • Client-supplied contractor scope and apparent-completion records
  • Removal, replacement, and documented-loss history
  • Material that can be shared with boards and qualified professionals
Verification boundary: SDPP records observable conditions and apparent completion. It does not certify technical correctness, code compliance, arboricultural standards, pesticide work, insurance coverage, or contractor performance beyond SDPP's qualifications.

2. Protection & Treatment

Licensed work can include site review, SAPW-aware monitoring, treatment planning, label-compliant application where appropriate, and an organized record of what was done.

Every application remains subject to the product label, site conditions, licensing scope, and job-specific preflight.

Clear professional boundaries

SDPP provides palm-focused treatment, monitoring, and documentation. Arboricultural, structural, laboratory, municipal, and tree-contracting decisions remain with the appropriately qualified provider or authority.

For public and urban-forestry partners, localized inventories, repeatable photo baselines, loss records, neighborhood context, and educational materials can supplement broader planning work. No official municipal role is implied.

3. Response, Removal & Replacement

When a palm changes quickly or preservation is no longer practical, the documentation should not stop.

Response

  • Urgent visible-change record
  • Earlier and current image comparison
  • Correspondence and decision history supplied by the owner
  • Questions for qualified contractors or licensed providers

Removal & Outcome

  • Pre-work photographs and client-supplied scope
  • Coordination with qualified outside contractors
  • Post-work and apparent-completion record
  • Documented-loss and property-history update

Replacement & Continuity

  • Replacement concepts
  • Specimen palm and cycad sourcing guidance
  • Logistics coordination
  • New baseline from installation forward
