I live inside the problem

I live in Old Escondido among the same mature Canary Island date palms I work to protect. I have captured adult South American palm weevils on my own property. I have treated my own declining CIDP, watched it over time, and compared its crown month after month instead of treating it as one stop on a distant route.

I also keep seeing the local pattern repeat: a strong mature crown, a subtle change in the center, rapid decline, then a stump. I photograph healthy palms, declining palms, removals, and the empty places left behind. That does not make every cause certain. It does make the problem personal and difficult to dismiss.

This is what local expertise means to me. It is not a slogan or a claim that nobody else can treat a palm. It is familiarity earned by being here, returning, and paying attention.

What I carry forward between visits

The pesticide application matters. So do the palm's identity, present condition, prior treatment, irrigation, nutrition, pruning, site access, and what changed since the last visit.

If I treat your palm, I want to recognize it when I return. I want a photograph of the whole crown, a treatment record, and enough continuity to notice when something no longer looks the same. If you call between visits because the center changed, I want that call while there may still be useful choices—so we can discuss whether an earlier visit is needed.

No local provider can guarantee that a palm will survive. I cannot see every concealed condition inside a crown, and treatment still has to fit the palm, site, and pesticide label. The advantage is not certainty. The advantage is having one nearby person who already knows the tree and owns the next conversation.

Knowing the local landscape

South American palm weevil is not an abstract future threat here. It is already changing San Diego landscapes. Mature palms that took decades to grow are being lost now.

That calls for more than a treatment invoice. It calls for someone who understands the local pest pressure, knows which palms are especially valuable and susceptible, notices the surrounding losses, and stays close enough to follow the work.

With SDPP, you speak directly with the person who visited the property, performed the treatment and kept the photographs. I can refer to that history when we discuss the next step.

I know the local palms because I live with them. I know the local problem because I am already in it.
