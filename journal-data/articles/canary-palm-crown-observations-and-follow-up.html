On September 13, I treated one Canary Island date palm in Encinitas with a soil drench. At the visit, I was not seeing the clearly upright uppermost frond structure I normally look for, while the rest of the canopy looked good. That observation left questions; it was not enough on its own to declare the palm beyond help.

Keep following the whole palm

The owner later reported decline. I returned and applied a limited spray at the base of the trunk. In October we continued comparing photographs and discussing changes in the crown and lower fronds. Those exchanges were part of follow-up, rather than a confirmed explanation for the changes or proof of a treatment result.

A photograph gives us a point to compare with the next view. Keep the earlier pictures, including ones taken before treatment. A whole-palm view shows the crown in relation to the lower canopy; a closer crown view helps show the new growth that caught your attention.

Tell me what changed and when

When you send me a palm photo, tell me when you first noticed the change and which palm it is. Try to photograph it from the same position as before. That makes a conversation about a particular frond or part of the crown much more useful than comparing unrelated angles.

If a change develops between visits, contact the person caring for the palm rather than waiting for the next planned service. A treatment visit is one point in the care of a palm. Following its condition over time helps us decide what needs another look.
