I treated a Canary Island date palm in Oceanside on August 26. The visit included an exterior treatment, a systemic soil drench, and baseline photographs of the whole palm, crown and surrounding site.

The next part of this story came through a check-in, rather than another treatment visit.

An October update from the homeowner

On October 9, I sent the owner a palm-care update and invited them to contact me with concerns. They replied on October 12: they had not noticed more weevils or pupal cocoons around the base, the top fronds appeared unchanged, and they had not seen yellowing.

That was useful information to add to the palm's history. It was also the homeowner's observation. I had not made a new on-site inspection in that exchange, so I could not turn the email into a pest clearance or a claim that the treatment had saved the palm.

Keeping the care going

The owner wanted to continue quarterly care. The next recommended treatment interval in the record is November 26; that date still needs to be coordinated as an appointment.

For me, this is a practical part of looking after a mature palm: do the work, keep a baseline, check in, and pay attention to what the owner notices between visits. A report that the crown looks unchanged is worth recording accurately, without adding a stronger conclusion than the evidence supports.

The August visit and October exchange give the next conversation something specific to work from. They tell us when treatment happened, what photographs were taken, and what the owner subsequently reported.

If you notice a change between visits, contact me about your palm rather than saving the question for a future appointment. You can also read about SDPP's annual palm protection program.
