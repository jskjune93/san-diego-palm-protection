Three palms on one property can call for three different conversations. I believe the condition of each one belongs at the center of the next decision.

On November 2, I completed a treatment visit for three Canary Island date palms at a San Diego County property. At the visit, I observed one dead palm, one suspect palm and one that appeared healthy.

Look at each palm separately

A count of three palms tells only part of the story. The condition of each one matters when discussing what comes next. In this group, the observations were different enough that describing all three simply as treated would leave out something important.

Suspect describes a concern. It does not explain the cause. Likewise, appearing healthy describes what I saw at that visit; it is not a finding about everything happening inside the palm.

Keep the care conversation specific

Prevention and a question about decline are different starting points. Tell me which palm concerns you and which palms you want to protect. If you have several Canaries, show me each one rather than assuming they all need the same conversation.

A whole-palm photograph and a closer crown view help identify the tree and the part you are watching. Keep earlier photographs, and tell me what changed and when. That gives the next discussion something specific to work from.

The November 2 observations are the starting conditions for this field note. They do not establish a later treatment result or explain why the dead or suspect palms reached those conditions.
