Palm Records. Monitoring. Protection. Response.

San Diego Palm Protection helps homeowners, HOAs, property managers, and organizations document the palms they manage, establish condition baselines, track change over time, verify completed work, coordinate response, and protect valuable landscape assets.

Available today: palm documentation, recurring monitoring, written reports, sourcing guidance, and coordination. Regulated treatment remains unavailable while licensing and required approvals are completed.

Current SDPP Scope

Documentation, monitoring, reporting, sourcing, and coordination are available now. Pesticide application, regulated pest-control treatment, palm pruning, removal, and installation are not currently offered.

One Property. One Portfolio. A Record That Holds Together.

Start with a dated baseline, return to the same palms and views, and preserve what changed, what outside work was supplied, and what happened next.

Palm Inventory & Condition Baseline

A numbered starting record with property references, standardized photographs, visible-condition observations, priority categories, and a useful follow-up interval.

Recurring Palm Monitoring

Quarterly, semiannual, or annual repeat documentation with side-by-side comparisons, notable changes, updated priorities, and questions for qualified providers.

Reports & Property Records

Organized palm-by-palm histories, dated photographic reports, removal and replacement records, supplied contractor documents, and next-step summaries.

Work & Outcome Verification

Pre-work and post-work photographs, a client-supplied scope, observable completion notes, unresolved discrepancies, and an updated palm history.

Observable documentation only—not certification of technical correctness, code compliance, or contractor performance.

Removal, Loss & Response

Document decline or loss, preserve correspondence and decision history, organize contractor questions, and record removal, replacement, and outcome.

Specimen Palms & Landscape Continuity

Specimen guidance, sourcing support, logistics coordination, replacement concepts, and a record that can follow a distinctive palm from installation forward.

The SDPP Approach

Palm decisions are clearer when the process starts with observation and ends with a thoughtful plan.

1

Document

Start with clear photos, visible condition notes, crown changes, and surrounding site context.

2

Understand

Consider the palm species, stress signs, SAPW awareness, neighborhood context, and whether the change is new or worsening.

3

Decide

The next step may be monitoring, preservation planning, removal decision planning, or replacement planning.

4

Steward

For valuable palms and mature landscapes, SDPP helps property owners think beyond a single photo and preserve long-term palm context.
