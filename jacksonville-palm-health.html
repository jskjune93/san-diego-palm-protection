Palm health & protection · Jacksonville area

Look at the problem before choosing a treatment.

Yellow leaves, a failing spear and crown decline can have different causes. A photo review can help identify the next question; it cannot confirm a disease or replace an inspection or laboratory test.

Bronzing and sudden decline

Early fruit drop, abnormal flower stalks and lower-frond bronzing warrant attention. Lethal bronzing affects susceptible date, sabal, queen and pindo palms. Similar symptoms can have other causes; testing may be needed. Symptomatic infected palms are not presented as curable.

Crown and weevil concerns

Tell me about spear damage, crown collapse or beetles, especially after transplanting, injury or stress. Florida’s native palmetto weevil, Rhynchophorus cruentatus, is one possibility in a broader health assessment.

Yellowing and poor growth

Potassium, magnesium, manganese and boron disorders can affect different leaves in different ways. Fertilizer history, irrigation, sandy soil and root conditions help explain what a palm may need.

What happens next

Some palms need care. Others need a different decision.

I start with the photos and history. We can then discuss local inspection, diagnostic testing referral, monitoring, nutrition planning, or a locally authorized treatment partner where appropriate. Bud rot, Ganoderma butt rot, Fusarium-related decline, drainage problems and root injury may require a different response. Some palms are unlikely to recover.

For healthy, valuable palms, ask about preventive planning and recurring monitoring. Local service availability and regulated work are confirmed separately before any commitment.

Was there recent freeze damage?

Brown foliage does not by itself establish whether a palm is lost. Note the date of the cold event and changes in new growth. Cold injury can be followed by delayed decline or secondary problems. Send photos before deciding on extensive pruning or removal.

If a crown is collapsing or a palm may fall, keep people clear and contact a local qualified tree professional. This inquiry is not an emergency response service.
