Who Owns Your Palm Treatment Company?

San Diego County | September 24, 2026

Why I would start with a local palm specialist, and what I would ask before hiring a private-equity-backed treatment company.

If I were hiring someone to protect a mature palm, I would want to know who owns the company. I would also want to know whether I could speak with that person after the first treatment.

A familiar local name does not always mean an independently owned local business. In San Diego’s tree and pest treatment market, some familiar names now belong to private-equity-backed groups.

That matters to me. When the work involves a palm that has been part of a property for decades, I would start with a qualified local specialist whose owner is still doing the work.

The name can stay after the ownership changes

Corky’s identifies Anticimex as its parent company. EQT Future acquired a controlling stake in Anticimex in 2021. Corky’s also advertises South American palm weevil treatment in San Diego. Corky’s ownership information · EQT’s account · Palm treatment service

SavATree is backed by Apax funds and operates in San Diego. Atlas Tree Service and Rancho Coastal Tree have both announced that they joined SavATree. Those three names belong to the same wider group. Apax’s investment announcement · San Diego branch · Atlas announcement · Rancho Coastal announcement

These are ownership facts, not findings about the quality of an individual crew. The people working locally may remain after an acquisition. Rancho Coastal’s announcement, for example, says its team would continue serving customers.

But the company’s name and the people on its route no longer tell you the whole story about who owns the business.

What the investor is buying

EQT describes pest control as attractive because it produces recurring revenue and offers opportunities to combine smaller businesses. Its account of Anticimex’s growth discusses acquisitions along existing routes to improve efficiency, customer density, and margins. It also describes investment in technology and a branch model intended to keep decisions close to customers. EQT’s account of Anticimex

There can be real benefits to that scale. My concern is what happens when the pressure to grow the business competes with the time an individual palm needs.

A fuller route can make service more efficient. It does not, by itself, tell you whether someone will compare this month’s crown with the last set of photographs. A recurring contract can keep treatment scheduled. It does not tell you who will reconsider the plan when the palm changes.

Those are the questions I would put ahead of the size of the company.

Where I would be cautious

I would be cautious about a treatment proposal that tells me more about the package than the person responsible for the palm.

I would want to know who checks its condition before treatment, who keeps its history, and who answers a concern between scheduled visits. If different people handle those jobs, I would want to know how the information moves between them.

I would also ask what the price includes, what costs extra, and what happens if the palm needs a different response. A renewal should be easy to understand. So should the work behind it.

Private-equity ownership gives me a reason to ask these questions carefully. It does not prove that a company rushes visits, recommends unnecessary treatment, or charges too much. I have not established those things about the firms named here. An independent business should have to answer the same questions.

Why I keep SDPP personal

I started San Diego Palm Protection after dealing with South American palm weevil activity and palm loss at my own property in Old Escondido. These are the palms I live among.

I visit the property, perform the treatment, take the photographs, and keep the service record. When you contact me about a change, you are speaking with the person who was there.

SDPP has to earn a living, too. The difference I can offer is direct responsibility for the work. You know who is treating the palm and who will discuss the next step with you.

No ownership structure can guarantee a palm’s survival. I still have to assess what is visible, acknowledge what I cannot see, and make sure any treatment fits the palm, the site, and the label.

For a mature palm that matters to you, my recommendation is to start with a local specialist you can speak with directly. Ask who owns the company. Then ask who will know your palm when it is time to return.
