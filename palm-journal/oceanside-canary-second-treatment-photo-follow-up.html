An Oceanside Return Visit: Following the Same Palm

November 5, 2026 | Oceanside, San Diego County · By John Krause

A November return visit in Oceanside, informed by August photographs and the questions the owner had raised.

A return visit should begin with some memory of the palm. I want the earlier photographs and the owner’s concerns in mind when I look at it again.

On November 1, I returned to an Oceanside property and completed a second treatment for its Canary Island date palm and cycads. The first treatment for the Canary was on August 26. By returning to the same palm, I could look at its current condition with the earlier visit in mind.

Follow the new fronds

I did not see anything immediately concerning during the November 1 follow-up. The owner had mentioned shorter or irregular fronds, so the crown and new frond growth remained specific things to watch. That visit observation was a reason to keep following the palm, not a finding that every question had been resolved.

After the visit, I sent the owner three side-by-side comparisons of the closest matching photographs from August 26 and November 1. Those views give us a useful starting point for the next comparison. Different angles and lighting can change how a crown looks, so I do not use a pair of photographs alone to explain why a palm changed.

Bring changes into the next conversation

I asked the owner to send photos or call if they noticed new changes or more weevil activity. A report from between visits helps identify what needs another look. When there is a particular frond you are watching, keep a whole-palm view and a closer crown view so we can find the same area again.

Continuing care means returning to the actual palm and keeping that conversation going. This November visit adds another dated look at the tree; what the next growth does is something to follow over time.
